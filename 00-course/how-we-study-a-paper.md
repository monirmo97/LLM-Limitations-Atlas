# How We Study a Paper

We do not read papers only to extract conclusions.

For every paper, answer these questions:

1. Why was this paper written?
2. What exact limitation or hypothesis does it test?
3. What models, datasets, tasks, and baselines were used?
4. What variable was changed or controlled?
5. What result was observed?
6. Is the result statistically and practically meaningful?
7. What alternative explanations remain?
8. How broad is the conclusion allowed to be?
9. What later work supports, weakens, or contradicts it?
10. What one sentence should remain in memory?

Never convert "a model failed in this experiment" into "LLMs cannot do X" without sufficient evidence.
